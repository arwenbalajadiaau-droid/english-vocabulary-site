01 · The Problem · Apple

Language-Teaching Problem: Poor/Limited Retention of English Vocabulary

Learning and retaining English vocabulary can be challenging for students. They may learn new words during a lesson but forget their meanings after a few days, especially when vocabulary is reviewed only once or studied through cramming before a test.

Alharthi (2015) explained that insufficient practice and other learning-related factors can contribute to vocabulary attrition, making newly learned words difficult to retain. Similarly, Uchihara et al. (2019) found that repetition plays an important role in incidental vocabulary learning, suggesting that repeated exposure to words can support vocabulary learning.

In addition, Kim and Webb (2022) found that spaced practice has positive effects on second-language learning, supporting the use of repeated review at different times rather than relying on one-time or cramming-based study.

These findings show the importance of providing students with regular, repeated, and spaced opportunities to review and practice English vocabulary to improve long-term retention.

📚 References

Alharthi, T. (2015). Reasons for vocabulary attrition: Revisiting the state of the art. International Education Studies, 8(10), 86–95. https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v8n10p86

Kim, S. K., & Webb, S. (2022). The effects of spaced practice on second language learning: A meta-analysis. Language Learning, 72(1), 1–34.

Uchihara, T., Webb, S., & Yanagisawa, A. (2019). The effects of repetition on incidental vocabulary learning: A meta-analysis of correlational studies. Language Learning, 69(4), 946–983.

02 · Our Solution · Sheena

Using Quizlet to Support Vocabulary Retention

Quizlet will be used as a learning tool for students to practice and learn new words. Students will look through digital flashcards with the target word, the definition of the word, the pronunciation of the word, and a sample sentence.

Students will then use Quizlet to practice the word further using the Learn, Write, Spell, and Test features, so that they can memorize the new words, get feedback, and review the words until they can remember them.

🔤

Recognize

Recognize 10–15 target vocabulary words and their definitions.

🧠

Remember

Practice repeatedly to strengthen recall and differentiate meanings.

✍️

Use

Use at least five target words correctly in original sentences.

🎯 Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Recognize and remember the definitions of 10–15 target vocabulary words.
  2. Differentiate the meanings of the words.
  3. Use at least five words correctly in a sentence.

📝 Procedure

1 · PRE-TESTMeasure students' initial vocabulary knowledge.
2 · INTRODUCEPresent the target vocabulary words.
3 · STUDYReview the Quizlet flashcards.
4 · PRACTICEUse Learn, Write, Spell, and Test repeatedly.
5 · APPLYUse selected vocabulary in sentences.
6 · POST-TESTMeasure the outcome of vocabulary retention.

06 · Application, Assessment & Integration · Ate Glai

Apply What You Learned

After studying on Quizlet, pick five new words you learned and write your own sentences using each word.

This activity gives you a chance to use the words in realistic situations and shows if you truly understand what each word means.

You can type directly into these boxes. Note: these fields are for completing the activity on the page; they do not automatically send answers to your teacher.

📊 Assessment

Students will take a short vocabulary test. The test will check if they can match the words to their correct definitions and use a few of them correctly in sentences.

We will compare their post-test scores with their pre-test scores to see if their word retention actually improved.

🔗 Integration / Expected Outcomes

By combining Quizlet practice, sentence writing, and testing, students should be able to remember 10 to 15 new words, tell their meanings apart, and correctly use at least five of them in sentences.

Comparing their pre-test and post-test scores will help show if using this digital tool really helped them hold on to new vocabulary.
